# LONGR — HIGH-CONVERTING ONBOARDING FUNNEL
## Coursiv-Inspired Structure, Rebuilt for “Eat for the Years Ahead”

**Document purpose:** Define a complete, screen-by-screen onboarding and conversion funnel for **Longr**, using the engagement architecture of the Coursiv onboarding funnel while rebuilding every question, message, psychological mechanism, personalization rule, and paywall element around Longr’s actual audience and value proposition.

**Core Longr positioning:**  
> **Eat for the years ahead.**

Longr is a health and longevity content platform for adults aged 40+ that helps people make better everyday food choices with the goal of supporting healthier aging, mobility, independence, heart health, brain health, metabolic health, strength, and quality of life.

This document does **not** simply rename Coursiv’s AI questions. It reconstructs the underlying conversion logic for Longr.

---

# 1. THE STRATEGIC IDEA

Coursiv’s onboarding works because the quiz is not merely a profile form.

It performs six jobs at once:

1. **Segmentation** — Who is this person?
2. **Problem awareness** — What are they worried about or struggling with?
3. **Goal formation** — What future outcome do they want?
4. **Commitment** — Are they willing to make a small daily investment?
5. **Personalization** — Can the product make the result feel specifically built for them?
6. **Conversion** — Can the paid offer be framed as the natural next step rather than a generic subscription?

Longr should use the same architecture.

The Longr onboarding should make the user feel:

> “This understands my age, what I care about, the food decisions I struggle with, and what I want my later years to look like.”

Then:

> “Longr has created a practical way for me to know what to eat, what to rethink, and what matters most for the years ahead.”

The funnel should therefore move through this emotional sequence:

**WHO I AM → WHAT I WANT TO PROTECT → WHAT I AM UNSURE ABOUT → WHAT COULD HAPPEN IF I IGNORE IT → WHAT IS STOPPING ME → WHAT I WANT TO CHANGE FIRST → WHAT I CAN COMMIT TO → WHO I WANT TO BE 10–20 YEARS FROM NOW → MY PERSONALIZED LONGR PLAN → PAYWALL**

---

# 2. CORE CONVERSION PRINCIPLES FOR LONGR

## Principle 1 — Sell the future, not “nutrition content”

Longr should rarely feel like:

> “Subscribe to read nutrition articles.”

It should feel like:

> “Know which everyday food choices deserve your attention now so you can eat for the years ahead.”

The user is not buying information for information’s sake.

They are buying:

- clarity,
- confidence,
- fewer avoidable food mistakes,
- a sense of control,
- better everyday decisions,
- and a practical way to support the future they care about.

---

## Principle 2 — The user should identify the problem themselves

Do not tell the user:

> “Your diet is bad.”

Ask questions that allow them to say:

- “Healthy eating feels confusing.”
- “I am not fully confident in what I eat.”
- “I worry some habits could catch up with me.”
- “I do not know what matters most after 50.”
- “There is too much conflicting advice.”
- “I want to make better decisions but I need a clear starting point.”

That is substantially more persuasive than Longr making an accusation.

---

## Principle 3 — Use future-self motivation

Longr’s strongest emotional territory is not simply disease avoidance.

It is the future the reader wants to preserve:

- walking independently,
- traveling,
- staying mentally sharp,
- keeping strength and mobility,
- spending meaningful time with children and grandchildren,
- remaining self-reliant,
- enjoying retirement rather than organizing life around illness,
- and reaching later decades with better health and quality of life.

This means the funnel should alternate between **risk awareness** and **positive future visualization**.

---

## Principle 4 — Strong emotion, medically credible claims

Longr can create urgency.

Longr should not imply:

- a particular food guarantees longer life,
- the subscription prevents a heart attack,
- a diet prevents dementia,
- a specific swap will keep someone out of hospital,
- or following Longr guarantees a particular medical outcome.

The offer is **information and practical guidance for healthier food choices**, not a medical diagnosis, treatment, or longevity guarantee.

---

## Principle 5 — One meaningful decision per screen

The quiz should feel fast even though it is long.

Each screen should contain:

- one headline,
- one short supporting sentence when needed,
- 3–6 large answer choices,
- visible progress,
- immediate transition after selection where appropriate.

Avoid long forms.

# PHASE 1 — BASIC PERSONALIZATION

# SCREEN 1 — AGE BRACKET

## Suggested screen headline

**How old are you?**

## Supporting copy

**We’ll use your answers to tailor Longr around the food choices that may matter more in your stage of life.**

## Suggested answer choices

- 40–49
- 50–59
- 60–69
- 70–79
- 80+
- I’m choosing for a parent or loved one

## What this screen is about

This is the easiest possible entry into Longr’s personalization funnel.

Age is central to Longr’s content architecture because the platform explicitly speaks to adults 40+, 50+, 60+, 70+, and people caring for older parents.

The question also immediately tells users:

> Longr is not generic nutrition content. It is interested in what matters at **my stage of life**.

## Product purpose

Age can influence:

- recommended article categories,
- examples used in headlines,
- content ordering,
- future-self language,
- strength/mobility content,
- heart and metabolic content,
- meal examples,
- and which “years ahead” framing is most resonant.

## Psychological purpose

The first screen should be extremely easy.

It creates an immediate micro-commitment.

Once a user taps their age group, they have started creating something personalized.

It also activates identity:

> “I am 57. This was made for people over 50.”

That recognition is powerful because Longr’s editorial strategy already depends heavily on the dream reader seeing themselves in the message.

## Personalization data captured

`age_bracket`

Possible values:

- `40_49`
- `50_59`
- `60_69`
- `70_79`
- `80_plus`
- `caregiver`

## How the answer should change the experience

A 44-year-old might later see:

> **Your plan for eating well in your 40s — and preparing for the decades ahead.**

A 67-year-old might see:

> **Your plan for staying strong, sharp, and independent through your 60s and beyond.**

A caregiver could see:

> **Your Longr plan for making better food choices with someone you care for.**

## Design / interaction notes

- Very large age-option cards.
- No free-text input.
- Auto-advance after selection.
- Progress label: **1 of 16**.
- Keep the tone simple; do not introduce fear yet.

## Credibility / safety guardrail

Do not imply that age alone determines what someone “must” eat.

Frame age as a **content personalization signal**, not a clinical assessment.

## Why this screen belongs first

This is Longr’s equivalent of Coursiv’s low-friction opening demographic question.

It creates momentum before asking anything emotional.

# SCREEN 2 — MAIN GOAL FOR THE YEARS AHEAD

## Suggested screen headline

**What matters most to you in the years ahead?**

## Supporting copy

**Choose the outcome you most want your everyday food choices to support.**

## Suggested answer choices

- Stay strong and mobile
- Protect my independence
- Support my heart health
- Stay mentally sharp
- Support healthier blood sugar
- Have more healthy years with the people I love

Optional final choice:

- I want to improve several of these

## What this screen is about

This is the central **desired-outcome segmentation question**.

Longr should not ask:

> “What nutrition topic are you interested in?”

That is too abstract.

The better question is:

> “What do you want your food choices to help protect?”

That connects nutrition to life.

## Product purpose

The answer becomes the user’s primary personalization axis.

Examples:

### If the user chooses “Stay strong and mobile”

Prioritize:

- protein-related education,
- meal quality,
- strength-supporting nutrition topics,
- mobility-oriented healthy aging articles.

### If the user chooses “Support my heart health”

Prioritize:

- sodium awareness,
- dietary pattern education,
- healthier breakfast and grocery choices,
- cooking-method articles.

### If the user chooses “Stay mentally sharp”

Prioritize:

- brain-health-oriented nutrition articles,
- overall dietary pattern education,
- healthy aging content.

### If the user chooses “Healthy blood sugar”

Prioritize:

- added sugar awareness,
- meal composition,
- label reading,
- practical swaps.

## Psychological purpose

This gets the user to articulate **why Longr matters to them**.

People are more motivated by:

> “I want to still travel at 72.”

than:

> “I want nutrition education.”

The question establishes an emotionally valuable outcome that can be reflected back throughout the funnel.

## Personalization data captured

`primary_future_goal`

## How the answer should change the experience

Later screens should repeat the selected goal.

Example:

> **You told us staying independent matters most to you.**

Then the result screen can say:

> **Your Longr plan will prioritize the everyday food choices most relevant to healthy aging, strength, and independence.**

## Design / interaction notes

Use small, tasteful icons:

- walking / strength,
- home / independence,
- heart,
- brain,
- metabolic/meal icon,
- family.

Avoid medical imagery such as hospital beds or alarming ECG graphics.

## Credibility / safety guardrail

Use language such as **support**, **help you make better choices**, and **healthy aging**.

Do not promise prevention or guaranteed outcomes.

## Why this screen exists here

Coursiv asks for the user’s main AI goal very early.

Longr should do the same with its highest-value emotional outcome.

## What this screen is about

Someone may ultimately want independence but currently be focused on high blood pressure.

## Product purpose

This gives Longr a practical content filter.

It can influence:

- home-feed ranking,
- push/email topics,
- article bundles,
- “Start here” recommendations.

## Psychological purpose

This makes Longr feel immediately relevant.

The user starts to think:

> “This is not going to dump hundreds of health articles on me. It is narrowing the information to what I care about.”

## Personalization data captured

`current_food_health_focus`

## How the answer changes the experience

If “blood pressure / sodium” is selected, the first content set might be:

- hidden sodium,
- breakfast choices,
- label reading,
- cooking methods,
- food swaps.

If “not sure” is selected, Longr should favor broad healthy-aging fundamentals.

## Design / interaction notes

Allow one primary choice.

Optional secondary prompt after selection:

> **You can change this later.**

That reduces commitment anxiety.

## Credibility / safety guardrail

This is **not** a diagnosis question.

Avoid asking:

> “What diseases do you have?”

unless Longr has a compelling, consent-driven reason and appropriate privacy practices.

## Why this screen exists here

Coursiv uses professional context to make its later recommendation feel tailored.

Longr needs an equivalent context layer that is relevant to food choices.

# 15. FINAL STRATEGIC POSITION

The strongest version of Longr’s onboarding does not sell “articles.”

It sells a progression:

> **From not knowing which food advice matters → to having a clear, age-relevant reading path for the future you want to protect.**

Coursiv’s core funnel logic works because users spend the onboarding constructing the argument for the product themselves.

Longr should do the same.

The user should reach the paywall already believing:

> **I care about my later years.**
>
> **Food choices are one area I can pay more attention to.**
>
> **I do not want more random information.**
>
> **I want clear guidance relevant to my age and goals.**
>
> **I can realistically spend a few minutes on this.**
>
> **Longr has already organized a path around what I told it.**

Only then should Longr ask the user to pay.

The final offer is not:

> **Subscribe to a health-content website.**

It is:

> **Unlock the plan you just built for the years ahead.**

# APPENDIX C — NON-NEGOTIABLE HEALTH LANGUAGE

## Longr can say

- Support heart health
- Support brain health
- Eat for healthier aging
- Make better everyday food choices
- Understand food habits associated with health risks
- Support better blood sugar management alongside appropriate care
- Reduce excess sodium
- Build healthier meals
- Stay informed about food choices that may matter more with age
- Protect the future you care about through more informed choices

## Longr should not promise

- Guaranteed longer life
- Guaranteed disease prevention
- A precise number of extra years
- A diagnosis based on quiz answers
- Treatment of a medical condition
- Hospital avoidance
- Certain protection from dementia, stroke, diabetes, cancer, or heart disease

Longr’s strongest brand position is:

> **Emotionally important. Practically useful. Medically credible.**

And the onboarding should make that visible from the first question to the final paywall.
