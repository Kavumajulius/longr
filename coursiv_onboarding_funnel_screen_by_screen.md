# Coursiv Web App Onboarding Funnel — Screen-by-Screen Breakdown

## Overview

Coursiv uses a long-form, quiz-driven onboarding funnel rather than a traditional 3–5 screen onboarding flow.

The current structure is approximately:

**16-question personalization quiz → Name capture → Personalized assessment → Before/after transformation → Email capture → Personalized selling/paywall page**

The funnel is designed to do more than collect profile information. It progressively gets the user to:

1. Identify their background.
2. Define what they want from AI.
3. Admit their current frustrations and skill gaps.
4. Recognize the risks of not learning AI.
5. Visualize a concrete outcome.
6. Commit time to learning.
7. Imagine the reward of succeeding.
8. Receive a “personalized” program.
9. Provide contact information.
10. Convert to a paid plan.

At a high level, the funnel follows this psychological sequence:

**Profile me → Make me acknowledge the problem → Make me define what I want → Make me commit → Show me the personalized solution → Charge me**

# Phase 1 — Basic Personalization

## Screen 1 — Age

### Main Question
**“How old are you?”**

Typical options include:

- 18–24
- 25–34
- 35–44
- 45–54
- 55+

### What this screen is about

This is the first segmentation screen. Coursiv begins with a very easy, low-friction question that almost anyone can answer instantly.

The screen also introduces the idea that the user’s answers will be used to personalize their AI learning program.

### Product Purpose

- Creates an easy first interaction.
- Gets the user clicking immediately.
- Establishes the personalization premise.
- Helps Coursiv segment users by age group.

### Psychological Purpose

The first action is deliberately simple. Once the user makes the first selection, they have already begun investing effort in the funnel.

## Screen 2 — Primary Goal

### Main Question
**“What is your main goal with AI?”**

Typical answer options include:

- Grow in my current role.
- Switch careers.
- Find freelance or side income.
- Work remotely or independently.

### What this screen is about

Coursiv asks the user to define the main outcome they want from learning AI.

### Product Purpose

This is one of the most important segmentation questions in the entire onboarding process.

Coursiv can later frame the personalized learning plan around the user’s selected goal.

For example:

- A career-growth user may see productivity and professional AI skills.
- A side-income user may see AI freelancing or monetization.
- A career-switching user may see AI skill-building and certification.

### Psychological Purpose

The user is not simply saying, “I want to learn AI.”

They are defining a more valuable personal outcome such as:

**“I want AI to help me earn more.”**

or

**“I want AI to improve my career.”**

That makes the eventual paid offer easier to position.

**“I don't have a clear roadmap”**

If they say:

**“I don't have enough time”**

If they say:

**“AI is too complicated”**

Coursiv promises beginner-friendly learning.

If they say:

**“I don't know where to start”**

Coursiv provides a guided starting point.

The user effectively tells Coursiv how to sell the solution back to them.

# The Funnel's Five Core Psychological Stages

Although there are approximately 21 visible stages when including post-quiz conversion screens, the entire funnel can be reduced to five strategic phases.

## 1. Profile Me

Coursiv learns:

- age,
- goal,
- industry,
- career level,
- AI experience.

The user begins believing that the final recommendation will be personalized.

---

## 2. Make Me Acknowledge the Problem

Coursiv asks about:

- overwhelm,
- low confidence,
- fear of falling behind,
- career anxiety,
- lack of roadmap,
- complexity,
- previous failed attempts.

This is critical.

Coursiv does not simply tell users:

**“You have a problem.”**

It asks questions that cause users to identify the problem themselves.

---

## 3. Make Me Define What I Want

Coursiv asks about:

- desired AI use case,
- first-week goal,
- career goal,
- desired reward.

The user begins building an image of a better future.

---

## 4. Make Me Commit

Coursiv asks the user to:

- finish a long questionnaire,
- choose a daily learning time,
- provide a name,
- provide an email.

Each action increases psychological investment.

This makes abandoning the process progressively less attractive.

---

## 5. Show Me the Personalized Solution and Sell It

Coursiv then presents:

- assessment results,
- personalized learning path,
- transformation,
- certificate positioning,
- paid program.

The product now feels like the logical solution to problems the user has already identified.

# Why Screens 4–8 Are Especially Important

Screens 4–8 form the **problem-awareness section** of the funnel.

They ask variations of:

- Are you overwhelmed?
- Are you confident?
- Are you afraid of falling behind?
- How experienced are you?
- What is holding you back?

These questions do more than personalize a curriculum.

They create a sequence where the user may internally conclude:

> AI is important.
>
> I am not where I want to be.
>
> I am struggling to learn it alone.
>
> I need structure.

Coursiv can then present itself as that structure.

This is one of the most important conversion mechanisms in the onboarding.

# Why Screens 14–16 Are Especially Effective

The last three quiz questions change the emotional direction of the funnel.

Earlier screens focus on the **problem**.

Screens 14–16 focus on the **future**.

## Screen 14
Defines a first achievable win.

## Screen 15
Gets the user to commit time.

## Screen 16
Gets the user to imagine the reward.

The sequence is effectively:

**Result → Commitment → Reward**

This moves the user from anxiety into optimism immediately before Coursiv reveals the personalized plan.

# Key Product Design Principles Used by Coursiv

## 1. One Question Per Screen

Coursiv avoids presenting a long form.

Each page generally contains one decision.

Benefits:

- lower cognitive load,
- mobile-friendly interaction,
- stronger feeling of momentum,
- higher apparent simplicity.

---

## 2. Visible Progress

The quiz communicates that the user is moving toward completion.

Near the end, users can see progress such as:

**15 / 16**

This encourages completion because abandoning the funnel when almost finished feels wasteful.

---

## 3. Progressive Commitment

The initial questions require almost no effort.

Later interactions require progressively more investment.

The order broadly moves from:

**Easy demographic answer → Emotional answers → Goals → Commitment → Personal details → Purchase**

---

## 4. Personalized Language

Coursiv repeatedly frames the result as belonging to the user.

Examples of the pattern include:

- Your goal
- Your focus
- Your readiness
- Your pace
- Your personalized program
- Your AI certificate program

This increases perceived relevance.

---

## 5. Pain Before Product

Coursiv does not immediately explain all product features.

The funnel first gets the user to articulate:

- frustration,
- uncertainty,
- fear,
- blockers,
- goals.

Only afterward does it strongly present the solution.

---

## 6. Concrete Short-Term Wins

Instead of promising only something vague like:

**“Master AI.”**

Coursiv also asks users to imagine practical early results.

For example:

- writing an effective prompt,
- automating a task,
- creating content,
- starting an AI-powered income activity.

This makes the learning journey feel attainable.

---

## 7. Future-Pacing

The user is repeatedly encouraged to imagine a future version of themselves who:

- understands AI,
- works faster,
- earns more,
- has stronger career prospects,
- receives an AI credential,
- feels confident using AI.

This future state gives the purchase emotional meaning beyond merely accessing course material.

# Strategic Funnel Formula

A simplified Coursiv-style onboarding formula is:

### Step 1 — Who are you?
Collect demographic and professional context.

### Step 2 — What do you want?
Identify the user's major outcome.

### Step 3 — What's stopping you?
Get the user to identify pain and blockers.

### Step 4 — What happens if you don't solve it?
Introduce urgency and consequences.

### Step 5 — What would success look like?
Define concrete outcomes.

### Step 6 — Will you commit?
Ask the user to choose a manageable daily action.

### Step 7 — Imagine the reward.
Future-pace the user into the desired result.

### Step 8 — Analyze their answers.
Present an assessment.

### Step 9 — Reveal the bridge.
Show the personalized program.

### Step 10 — Capture contact details.
Collect name and email.

### Step 11 — Present the offer.
Convert the user into a paid customer.

# Final Product Insight

Coursiv's onboarding is not merely an onboarding experience.

It performs several jobs simultaneously:

- user research,
- segmentation,
- personalization,
- problem amplification,
- goal setting,
- objection handling,
- commitment building,
- lead capture,
- value demonstration,
- conversion.

The most important insight is that **the quiz itself is part of the sales experience**.

Coursiv does not wait until the paywall to sell the product.

Each question gradually constructs the argument for why the user needs the product.

By the time the user reaches the paid offer, the funnel has already established:

**I want AI skills → I am currently struggling → AI matters to my future → I need a structured way to learn → this program was built around my needs.**

That is the central conversion logic behind the onboarding funnel.
